Muhamed Isabegović is a staff engineer in Vienna by way of Tuzla, Bosnia and Herzegovina. He brought EuRuKo to Sarajevo in 2024, the first Ruby conference ever held in the country, then founded RubyConf Austria. He tells Joel and Greg how pitching his hometown on a wave of homesickness turned into three days, three tracks and €100,000 he had to raise, why he organizes conferences to put himself in a room with people he wants to learn from, and the story of the one goat that fed all of Tuzla.

That goat is now the name of GoatConf, a free, tech-agnostic conference in Tuzla on 3 to 5 June 2027, with an hour-long break after every session for the hallway track. They talk about why small towns work for conferences, whether free tickets mean empty seats (and 37signals' trick of a $100 deposit you get back when you show up), and what makes a speaker lineup: the people on the stage, not the stage.

Then the AI side of Muhamed's work. Chasing Chad Fowler's ideas about regenerative software, he added deterministic constraints for Ruby and Rails to Enola, Dejan Menges's architecture graph, so a check catches each mistake an agent makes. He describes Tabula, his memory layer and harness, and ∗codeplain's idea that code gets regenerated, not maintained. Greg has had little luck with open models, and Joel connects it all to Sam Ruby's Roundhouse.

To close, Greg flags Omarchy 4.5 and this week's Rails security releases (upgrade, especially if you use Active Storage), and the three of them dig into a Railway employee's agent probing a competitor's platform: who is responsible when an agent goes off the rails, how Greg uses AI in security audits, and whether you can still sell software anyone can build, or only the expertise behind it.
