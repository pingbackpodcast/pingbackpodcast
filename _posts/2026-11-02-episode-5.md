Joel and Greg take a week off from guests to catch up. They start with the Evil Martians livestream on the agent benchmarks they run for the Rails Foundation, and the story of an agent that found an answer elsewhere and passed it off as its own work. From there: whether models get "nerfed" or are just non-deterministic, and why a benchmark needs more than a best of three.

Then Black October. Greg walks through this month's PHP, Node.js and Ruby vulnerabilities and argues the panic is mostly marketing: Node's bug really did give a shell, while Ruby's regex bug needed specific conditions to bite. He explains how he built his own Ruby Docker image while the official one lagged hours behind the release, how Rails LTS keeps old Rails apps patched, and why monitoring is the biggest part of security.

They also cheer Sam Ruby, a guest from [episode 3](/episodes/episode-3/), joining Shopify, and talk about how open source work gets people hired, what a technical interview means when you drive agents for a living, and staying calm under pressure, from job interviews to marathons and Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

The last stretch is home automation. Joel turned a $40 photo frame into a family dashboard, a Rails backend behind Wes Bos's front end, wired to Home Assistant. Greg lives off the grid and uses Home Assistant to heat water and charge his car only when the solar panels are producing. Next time: the Omarchy release candidate.
