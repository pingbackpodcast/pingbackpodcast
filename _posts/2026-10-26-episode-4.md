Joel and Greg talk with Josef Strzibny, the Ruby developer advocate at SerpApi and the author of *Deployment from Scratch*, *Kamal Handbook* and *Test Driving Rails*. A few weeks after his Kamal deep dive at Rails World in Austin, Josef talks about what a developer advocate actually does, judging a 700-entry hackathon, and why the projects with AI-generated demo videos were the hardest to love.

Most of the episode is about Kamal: what it is, what it deliberately leaves out, and why most apps don't need autoscaling. Josef explains why he'd move web and job servers to Kamal but keep a managed database, when a PaaS still makes sense for a team, and the spots where new users trip up, from customer subdomains on a SaaS to Cloudflare's SSL modes. He also floats an idea he's been sitting on: a Kamal distribution, the way Kubernetes has them.

Then the business side: selling more than $100k of self-published books on Gumroad, why both Josef and Greg have soured on the platform, and the open source, self-hosted Rails shop Greg moved his products to. The conversation ends on AI: why tokens only look free, what real prices might have meant for junior developers, and using AI to build tools instead of burning tokens on chores.

Greg also flags this week's Ruby security release, which fixes regex engine bugs behind four GitLab CVEs. If your app builds regular expressions from user input, upgrade.
