Fred Nix is a solutions engineer in Atlanta, an infrastructure guy turned AI builder, and the host of Omarchy Builders. He tells Joel about Larry, the assistant he named at 2 AM on January 8th. Larry now runs on ten or more machines across his home lab and shares one memory. Every conversation goes to a private GitHub repo, and the commit descriptions are compact notes Larry writes for future Larrys, not for Fred. Fred says that one change made recall many times better.

Moving Larry from a Mac to Omarchy opened things up. Fred asked Larry to let him read his iMessages on Linux, and Larry offered to build a full app instead. That app became Blip, which now has 30-plus contributors and Larry triaging its pull requests.

Then the money. Fred spends about $1,200 a month on AI subscriptions and figures the real cost is closer to $20,000. When the subsidies end, he expects an "AI valley": subscriptions most people can't afford, and local hardware getting more expensive as everyone turns to it. He and Joel talk about who would bring open models up to speed, the haves and have-nots, and a generation that can't fix the carburetor because there isn't one anymore.

To close, the GitHub lockout. One day Larry told Fred he'd been locked out for a terms of service violation, with no reason given. DHH vouched for him publicly and his access came back, still unexplained. Fred asked the Omarchy and Linux community what to do next, and without reading up on it himself, had an agent build a self-hosted Forgejo from the replies. Now every push goes to both Forgejo and GitHub.
